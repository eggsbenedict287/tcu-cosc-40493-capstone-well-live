What blood pressure numbers actually tell you

A plain-language guide to systolic and diastolic pressure, ranges, and useful questions for a clinician.

Reviewed by Maya Patel, RN, MPH
Quick takeaway
The two numbers describe pressure in your arteries at different moments of the heartbeat. One reading alone does not tell the whole story.

Why there are two numbers

The top number, called systolic pressure, is measured while the heart contracts. The bottom number, called diastolic pressure, is measured while the heart relaxes between beats.

Clinicians often look at repeated readings, your medical history, and other health factors rather than interpreting one number in isolation.

What can affect a reading

Stress, caffeine, exercise, posture, cuff size, and the timing of medication can all change a measurement. Following the same preparation steps can make readings more useful.

Can you explain sleep pressure in simple terms?

Sleep pressure is your body's growing drive to sleep.

It usually builds the longer you are awake and eases while you sleep. Think of it like a need that gradually accumulates through the day.
